Strive components

The pieces every version 1 screen is built from, in every state, made only of the tokens in 15-tokens/tokens.css. Strings are Swedish where they are longer than English.

Components

Every component in every state that applies to it, built from the decisions below and Apple's defaults where nothing was decided. Press anything; − and + work; the timer counts down. A state that does not apply says why. Judge sizes on the phone: the desktop browser misleads.

Glass cannot show its Reduce Transparency fallback here: Safari does not report the setting. In the app, every glass surface turns solid (surface in light mode, surface-container-high in dark) when it is on.

Apple HIG review

Run on this page with apple-hig-review on 2026-10-20. Each rule below comes from a HIG page read that day; the rest is marked judgment.

  • Tap areas are 44 × 44 pt. Accessibility: default 44 × 44 pt, minimum 28 × 28 pt. Small capsules are 36 pt tall to the eye with a 44 pt tap area; round steppers and title-bar buttons are 44 pt.
  • Every button has a press state. Buttons: Always include a press state for a custom button. It starts on touch-down (Q2).
  • One filled button per view; destructive is never filled. Buttons: keep prominent buttons to one or two per view, and do not give the primary role to a destructive action.
  • Waiting shows a spinner and a specific label. Buttons (iOS) puts the activity indicator in the button with a changed label, like Exporterar…; Progress indicators: avoid vague words like loading, so the GPS spinner says what it is looking for.
  • Fields have a label above and a hint inside; search has a clear button; numbers use the decimal keypad and the locale's format. Text fields, all four.
  • Steppers sit with a field and change an obvious value. Steppers: pair a stepper with a field when large changes are likely. I3 shows them while the field is being edited.
  • Rows that open something have a chevron; choices get a checkmark. Lists and tables: a disclosure indicator to drill in, a checkmark for a selected option.
  • Segmented controls: up to five segments, nouns, equal widths. Segmented controls.
  • Sheets: a grabber, Cancel on the leading edge, one sheet at a time, swipe to dismiss. Sheets (iOS).
  • An alert only for an uncommon action that cannot be undone; an action sheet for choices after an intentional one. Alerts. Ta bort is not red: Carl chose to delete, and Alerts reserves the destructive style for a result people did not deliberately choose. Släng passet is red: he tapped Avsluta, not discard.
  • Charts: short axis labels, units in the title, grid lines light. Charts. The selected bar is teal and its value is written in the title, so colour is never the only signal; the chart has a spoken summary.
  • Tabs are never disabled or hidden; labels are single words; the bar floats on glass. Tab bars.
  • Back is the standard chevron without text; Done is prominent and trailing; tab roots use a large title. Toolbars.
  • Reduce Motion drops the press's scale and keeps the dim. Accessibility. Judgment: the spinner keeps turning, because a still indicator reads as a stalled task (Progress indicators).

Sources opened: accessibility, buttons, text-fields, steppers, lists-and-tables, segmented-controls, sheets, alerts, progress-indicators, charts, tab-bars, toolbars.

Deviations from Apple's guidelines

Accepted for now, because Carl is the only user. Listed so they are cheap to fix if Strive is ever submitted to the App Store.

  • Start is an action in the tab bar (from strive#14). Apple, Tab bars: Use a tab bar to support navigation, not to provide actions. The fix is a Start button in the title bar of Home and Plan.

Decided

  • Components: buttons, inputs, list rows, cards, stat tiles, chart style, timer, dialogs, navigation bars (the tab bar and the title bar), plus a segmented control, a sheet and an empty state. The last three are each needed by at least two version 1 screens (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q1).
  • A press shrinks and dims (P2): buttons and segments scale to 96 % and spring back, rows turn grey, feedback starts on touch-down. With Reduce Motion on, a press only dims (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q2).
  • Button corners (B3): shape-large (16 pt) on full-width buttons, a capsule on small buttons in a row. One filled button per view (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q3).
  • Two list styles, chosen by what the row is (rule A): a long list or a set of choices is a block with hairlines, split into groups where the data has them (History by week, Library by muscle, Settings, Profile, Onboarding, Export). A short list you start, edit or reorder is joined tiles (templates in Plan, exercises in a template, the sets in Active workout). One thing with its own button is a card. Separate tiles (L2) are not used (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q4).
  • Number input (I3): a grey field with the number in headline-medium and last time’s value as a grey hint; − and + appear while it is being edited, in 2,5 kg and 1 rep steps. Weight and reps fit side by side (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q5).
  • Numbers and charts (D1): a tile per number, label above, value in display-small (34 pt) with a small unit. Chart marks are grey; the selected bar or point is teal. Units go in the chart's title, axis labels stay short (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q6).
  • Rest timer (T5 in glass, K2): a glass card floating over the set list, inset from the edges and the bottom, with a 64 pt amber ring, the time in display-small (34 pt), round − and + with the 15 s step between them, and Hoppa över as its own capsule at the far right so it is not hit by mistake. Glass because it floats over content (G2). Judged on the phone, where it looked best; the desktop browser misled on size (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q7).
  • Everything Q1–Q7 left open follows Apple's defaults, as the catalogue draws it: the tab bar, title bars, sheet, alert and action sheet, segmented control, text field, empty state and activity indicator. Approved on the phone (Carl, 2026-10-20).
  • The timer is a component although only Active workout uses it. The issue's rule is two screens; Carl keeps it (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q1).

Considered: the rest timer (T5 in glass with K2 chosen)

Newest: T5 in glass, as G2 decided for things that float over content, with two control sets. K1 joins −15 s and +15 s into one capsule; K2 uses round − and + around the step. Hoppa över is a capsule of its own at the far right in both.

Before that: T4 and T5, Carl's strip as a floating card. −15 and +15 sit together on the left; Hoppa över is a text button at the far right, so it is not hit by mistake.

Rest starts by itself when a set is logged, counts down from your default rest, and ends with a sound and a buzz on the phone. The countdown below is live and loops. Progress is amber, the tertiary role, so teal keeps meaning "tap". Each option has −15 s, +15 s and Hoppa över.

T5 in glass · K1 joined capsule

One capsule for −15 s and +15 s with a hairline between, the way iOS draws a stepper. Hoppa över is a separate capsule, same height, as far from them as the card allows.

T5 in glass · K2 round buttons

Round − and + of 40 pt with the step between them, like a volume control. Shortest labels; the step size is said once.

T4 · Floating card, bar

T2's content in a card like the rest of the app: 16 pt corners, inset from the edges and the bottom, time in 34 pt over an amber bar. Solid, with the glass shadow so it lifts off the list.

T5 · Floating card, small ring

The same card with a 64 pt ring instead of the bar, and the time in 34 pt beside it. The ring reads as a clock at a glance; the time stays as large as in T4.

T1 · Ring card

A card in the set list with a ring and the time in 56 pt, like the Clock app's timer. Readable from a bench; it pushes the next sets down while you rest.

T2 · Strip above the button

A solid strip pinned to the bottom of the screen: time, a bar, the buttons. The set list keeps its place. Smaller time, still 34 pt.

T3 · Glass pill

A floating glass pill over the list (glass on controls, as G2 decided), its fill drains as rest runs out. The most compact; tap it to get −15/+15. Time in 22 pt is the hardest to read from a distance.

Considered: numbers and charts (D1 chosen)

Stat tiles carry summaries, the live run and Progress; the chart style carries Progress and the run summary. Same workout and the same eight weeks in each option. The chart's units sit in its title and its axis labels stay short, as Apple's Charts page asks for small screens. Tap a bar or point to select it.

D1 · Tiles, grey bars

Each number is its own tile: label above, value in 34 pt, unit small. Bars are grey; the selected week is teal, so teal still means "selected", as the tokens decided. Close to Apple's Fitness and Health.

D2 · One summary card, teal bars

The four numbers share one card, value above label, like Strava's summary. Every bar is teal and the selected one stronger. Livelier, but teal then also means "data", which the tokens page ruled out.

D3 · Numbers as rows, a line

Label left, value right, in a block (rule A). The chart is a line with points; the selected point is teal. Densest, reads like Settings; the numbers lose their weight.

Considered: entering weight and reps (I3 chosen)

The number input logs every set, between sets, often one-handed. Each option shows the default, the field being edited, an error, and disabled (a set you have not reached). The field opens the phone's decimal keypad; the browser cannot show it, so a tap here only focuses. 2,5 kg steps, Swedish decimal comma.

I1 · Stepper beside the number

− and + either side of the value; tap the value to type. Apple's Steppers page pairs a stepper with a field for exactly this. Adjusting last time's 80 kg by one step is one tap. The cost: 82,5 and two steppers do not fit in half an iPhone's width, so weight and reps take a row each.

I2 · Field only, last time as a hint

A grey field with the number large; empty fields show last time's value in grey, and logging accepts it as it is. Compact enough for weight and reps side by side in one row, as Strong does. A change always means typing.

I3 · Field, steppers while editing

I2 at rest; − and + appear while the field is being edited. Compact in a set list, quick when you are changing it. The steppers are hidden until you tap, so they are found by trying.

Considered: how list rows group (rule A chosen: L1 and M2 are one block style, M1 the tiles)

Rows carry History, Plan, You, the exercise library and Settings. A card holds one thing with its own action, like today's planned workout, and sits above the rows in every option. Numbers follow the locale: a decimal comma and a space between thousands in Swedish.

L1 · Inset grouped, like Settings

Rows share one white block with 16 pt corners and hairlines between them. iOS's default for grouped content; the card and the block look like the same family.

L2 · Every row a tile

Each row is its own rounded tile with a gap, as the 14-navigation wireframes drew them. Each activity reads as an object; long lists take more height.

L3 · Edge to edge, like Mail

Rows run the full width with hairlines, no corners. Densest; suits a long history, and the card above is the only rounded object.

M1 · Joined tiles (L1 + L2)

Every row is its own tile, 4 pt apart, but only the group's outer corners are round (16 pt); the inner ones are 6 pt. Reads as one group and as separate activities at once. Android 16's grouped lists look like this; iOS draws hairlines instead.

M2 · One block per group (L1 + L2)

L1's block, but each week is its own tile with space between, so a long history breaks into objects. Plain iOS: Settings does the same with its sections.

Considered: button corners (B3 chosen)

The hierarchy is fixed by Apple's Buttons page: one filled button for the most likely action, tinted for the rest, text buttons for small actions, and red text for something destructive. What is open is the corner. The text button's dashed outline shows its 44 × 44 pt tap area.

B1 · Rounded rectangle, 12 pt

What the tokens page sketched: shape-medium on every button. Reads as a card's sibling; steady in a vertical stack.

B2 · Capsule everywhere

shape-full on every button: iOS 26's look for buttons on glass. Softest; a wide capsule at the bottom of a screen reads as one target from a distance.

B3 · Rounded wide, capsule small

shape-large (16 pt) on full-width buttons, capsule on small ones in a row. Apple's own split for stacks and rows (stated on the Buttons page for visionOS).

Considered: what a press looks like (P2 chosen)

Every tappable component inherits this. Press and hold each button, row and segment; the right-hand copy is frozen in its pressed state. Feedback starts on touch-down, as Apple's motion guidance asks.

P1 · Dim, like iOS

Buttons and segments fade to half strength while held; rows turn grey. Fades back in 250 ms. What UIKit buttons and table rows do, and the calmest of the three.

P2 · Shrink and dim

Buttons and segments shrink to 96 % and spring back; rows turn grey as in P1. Closer to iOS 26's Liquid Glass controls, and the most physical. With Reduce Motion on, it falls back to P1.

P3 · Ripple, like Android

A 12 % layer of the text colour, plus a ring that spreads from the finger. Material 3's state layer; it reads as Android on an iPhone.
