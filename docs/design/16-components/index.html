strive#16 · Core components

Strive components

The pieces every version 1 screen is built from, in every state, made only of the tokens in 15-tokens/tokens.css. Strings are Swedish where they are longer than English.

Deciding: Q5, entering weight and reps

The number input logs every set, between sets, often one-handed. Each option shows the default, the field being edited, an error, and disabled (a set you have not reached). The field opens the phone's decimal keypad; the browser cannot show it, so a tap here only focuses. 2,5 kg steps, Swedish decimal comma.

I1 · Stepper beside the number

− and + either side of the value; tap the value to type. Apple's Steppers page pairs a stepper with a field for exactly this. Adjusting last time's 80 kg by one step is one tap. The cost: 82,5 and two steppers do not fit in half an iPhone's width, so weight and reps take a row each.

I2 · Field only, last time as a hint

A grey field with the number large; empty fields show last time's value in grey, and logging accepts it as it is. Compact enough for weight and reps side by side in one row, as Strong does. A change always means typing.

I3 · Field, steppers while editing

I2 at rest; − and + appear while the field is being edited. Compact in a set list, quick when you are changing it. The steppers are hidden until you tap, so they are found by trying.

Decided

  • Components: buttons, inputs, list rows, cards, stat tiles, chart style, timer, dialogs, navigation bars (the tab bar and the title bar), plus a segmented control, a sheet and an empty state. The last three are each needed by at least two version 1 screens (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q1).
  • A press shrinks and dims (P2): buttons and segments scale to 96 % and spring back, rows turn grey, feedback starts on touch-down. With Reduce Motion on, a press only dims (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q2).
  • Button corners (B3): shape-large (16 pt) on full-width buttons, a capsule on small buttons in a row. One filled button per view (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q3).
  • Two list styles, chosen by what the row is (rule A): a long list or a set of choices is a block with hairlines, split into groups where the data has them (History by week, Library by muscle, Settings, Profile, Onboarding, Export). A short list you start, edit or reorder is joined tiles (templates in Plan, exercises in a template, the sets in Active workout). One thing with its own button is a card. Separate tiles (L2) are not used (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q4).
  • The timer is a component although only Active workout uses it. The issue's rule is two screens; Carl keeps it (Carl, 2026-10-20, Q1).

Considered: how list rows group (rule A chosen: L1 and M2 are one block style, M1 the tiles)

Rows carry History, Plan, You, the exercise library and Settings. A card holds one thing with its own action, like today's planned workout, and sits above the rows in every option. Numbers follow the locale: a decimal comma and a space between thousands in Swedish.

L1 · Inset grouped, like Settings

Rows share one white block with 16 pt corners and hairlines between them. iOS's default for grouped content; the card and the block look like the same family.

L2 · Every row a tile

Each row is its own rounded tile with a gap, as the 14-navigation wireframes drew them. Each activity reads as an object; long lists take more height.

L3 · Edge to edge, like Mail

Rows run the full width with hairlines, no corners. Densest; suits a long history, and the card above is the only rounded object.

M1 · Joined tiles (L1 + L2)

Every row is its own tile, 4 pt apart, but only the group's outer corners are round (16 pt); the inner ones are 6 pt. Reads as one group and as separate activities at once. Android 16's grouped lists look like this; iOS draws hairlines instead.

M2 · One block per group (L1 + L2)

L1's block, but each week is its own tile with space between, so a long history breaks into objects. Plain iOS: Settings does the same with its sections.

Considered: button corners (B3 chosen)

The hierarchy is fixed by Apple's Buttons page: one filled button for the most likely action, tinted for the rest, text buttons for small actions, and red text for something destructive. What is open is the corner. The text button's dashed outline shows its 44 × 44 pt tap area.

B1 · Rounded rectangle, 12 pt

What the tokens page sketched: shape-medium on every button. Reads as a card's sibling; steady in a vertical stack.

B2 · Capsule everywhere

shape-full on every button: iOS 26's look for buttons on glass. Softest; a wide capsule at the bottom of a screen reads as one target from a distance.

B3 · Rounded wide, capsule small

shape-large (16 pt) on full-width buttons, capsule on small ones in a row. Apple's own split for stacks and rows (stated on the Buttons page for visionOS).

Considered: what a press looks like (P2 chosen)

Every tappable component inherits this. Press and hold each button, row and segment; the right-hand copy is frozen in its pressed state. Feedback starts on touch-down, as Apple's motion guidance asks.

P1 · Dim, like iOS

Buttons and segments fade to half strength while held; rows turn grey. Fades back in 250 ms. What UIKit buttons and table rows do, and the calmest of the three.

P2 · Shrink and dim

Buttons and segments shrink to 96 % and spring back; rows turn grey as in P1. Closer to iOS 26's Liquid Glass controls, and the most physical. With Reduce Motion on, it falls back to P1.

P3 · Ripple, like Android

A 12 % layer of the text colour, plus a ring that spreads from the finger. Material 3's state layer; it reads as Android on an iPhone.
